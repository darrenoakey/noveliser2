## ACT I — Stranded (Chapter 1, §1–3)

- **Status quo image (§1):** Elena measures a derelict Tremé jazz hall by phone-light, cataloguing what’s worth saving as the storm rolls in. Her voice memos are immaculate, her contingencies alphabetized, and her patience with the airline’s cheerful rebooking app considerably less so. Her flight home is cancelled before Monday’s career-defining partnership pitch, and every rebooking fails. **BUT**
- **INCITING INCIDENT (§2):** In Camille’s rain-lashed hotel bar, Marcus is corrupting the house piano with a reharmonized standard. Whisky-fueled banter ignites—“You hate jazz?” “I hate lazy resolutions”—and he begins turning her objections into rhythms, forcing her to argue in tempo. Vivian watches from the corner, tour contract in her bag, clocking the attraction as a scheduling risk. **THEREFORE**
- **PLOT POINT ONE (§3):** With sixty guests stranded and Sunday’s jazz broadcast on the books, Camille conscripts them both—Elena’s logistics, Marcus’s piano—locking them into a shared weekend project. The hotel staff begins reserving them a small worktable labeled **PRODUCTION MEETING**, despite mounting evidence that no production meeting requires whisky, candlelight, or quite so much eye contact. A piano lesson becomes an exercise in trust: Marcus guides Elena’s hands without taking over, and their almost-kiss makes retreat impossible. Monday’s colliding deadlines—her pitch in Chicago, his tour signature—become the story’s ticking engine.

## ACT II — The Electric Weekend (Chapters 2–3, §4–9)

- **THEREFORE** a counterfeit domesticity blooms (§4): napkin drafting, shared staff meals at the increasingly well-dressed “production meeting” table, and a second-line detour between squalls. Elena can calculate the load capacity of the ballroom balcony but cannot stop counting long enough to dance—until Marcus matches her rigid little two-step and gradually leads her somewhere unplanned. Their flirtation escalates through small acts of attention: he remembers how she takes her coffee; she keeps his annotations instead of redrafting the napkin clean. **BUT Disaster 1:** the power grid fails, threatening the Sunday broadcast that funds Camille’s staff bonuses. Elena builds generator logistics by flashlight—love as labor, her old religion—while wrestling with Camille’s ancient generator, **Bessie**, whose laminated startup procedure ends with an undocumented final step known only to maintenance.
