The white Persian regarded the scene as if it were an unfortunate performance piece that had run slightly long. Its gaze held nothing of demand, merely recognition—the kind a creature affords an expensive fixture it has decided, for its own private amusement, to inhabit momentarily. Paris allowed her fingers to remain poised over the frequency modulator dial, refusing to acknowledge the animal's silent critique. It was a deliberate act of professional recalibration; she would not let triviality dictate the terms of engagement.

"The concept is beautiful," Paris finally conceded, not looking at the cat but letting her eyes rest on Q’s flushed face, which still glowed with the zeal of intellectual discovery. "To turn human existence into a broadcast signal. To make regret quantifiable." She allowed herself a faint smirk, a brief flash of dry amusement that only she could appreciate.

Q swelled with pleased exhaustion. “It is absolute control through understanding. A pure science. You eliminate variables by defining them—by quantifying the moment of subjective failure.” He gestured dramatically toward the glowing piece of equipment, as if presenting a solved equation to an admiring chorus.

Alistair coughed, a dry, theatrical sound that achieved maximum cynical resonance. “Ah, so Laporte has built himself a perfect crystal ball for moral judgment. Flawless prediction, impeccable data acquisition. And what does his pristine network exclude from its calculus?” He finally directed his attention to the cat, which had shifted into an unnervingly elegant loaf position near the pedestal's base. The animal looked bored with the entire operation.

“The unpredictable variables,” Paris finished for him, her voice dropping in register so that it forced everyone—Q, Alistair, even the ambient noise of the bustling market—to lean slightly closer to hear her. She didn’t need to look at the cat; she simply felt its presence pulling at the delicate scaffolding of her concentration.

She finally shifted her focus back to the oxidized brass dial. The organic residue remained like a stubborn argument against physics, a flaw in Laporte's immaculate design. It needed identification—a chemical fingerprint, an emotional parallel. She reached into the small velvet pouch she wore concealed within her fitted jacket sleeve and extracted a hypo-dermic sample vial filled with reagent.

“The challenge isn’t just intercepting the signal,” Paris murmured, speaking to herself more than to anyone present. “It is determining what kind of ‘noise’ Laporte considers background static.” She gently dabbed the residue using specialized blotting paper attached to the vial's tip. The yellowed material dissolved slightly into a hazy suspension within the glass cylinder.

Q leaned in instantly, abandoning his lectures on signal harmonics and becoming a focused, professional scientist again. He peered at the liquid under his own portable UV lamp. “This… this is unusual. It’s complex. I detect traces of high-grade bio-polymers—very rich in protein structure, not mineral origin. And there’s something else. A fibrous compound, almost silk-like, mixed with… glandular secretions? Wait.”

Q paused, peering closer at the sample bowl. His head snapped up, his eyes widening slightly behind thick lenses. He seemed to experience a rare moment of profound cognitive pause—the kind that happens when logic crashes into inexplicable biology.

“It’s layered,” he continued, tapping a finger against the polished metal table edge. “The structure suggests repeated external accumulation in a confined space—a micro-environment with high metabolic turnover rates. Perhaps… grooming residue?” He blinked rapidly, as if realizing how absurd his own hypothesis was. Then, catching himself, he regained his composure. “A biological indicator of routine habitation. From the immediate vicinity of the source.”

Paris withdrew her hand slowly, taking a moment to let the faint scent—ozone mixed with velvet and wet fur—settle in the air. It was an absurd detail for men discussing quantum control systems, yet it felt profoundly significant. The meticulous capture of this single piece of data was infinitely more valuable than all of Q’s jargon combined; it pointed to *proximity* and *sustained occupation*.

“So,” Paris summarized, her voice now dangerously even, carefully smoothing out any emotional echo of annoyance or curiosity. “Laporte's machine is not merely recording the abstract signal of human emotion. It requires a physical anchor point—a source constantly present in its operational range.” She cast a cool glance at the pristine white cat, which had finally lifted itself entirely onto the edge of the pedestal, directly beside the humming modulator dial.

The cat sat down fully, settling into an authoritative pose that suggested this entire conversation was merely background static to its afternoon nap schedule. Its tail gave one imperceptible twitch—a flicker that seemed charged with silent commentary on human inadequacy.

Alistair watched the exchange unfold, his cynical posture dissolving slightly, replaced by genuine surprise. He observed Paris’s controlled engagement with the mundane fluff of a cat and Q’s frantic, academic interest in molecular residue. It was entirely out of character for high-stakes espionage drama. It felt… domestic. And precisely that unexpected quality unsettled him.

“You are obsessed with the detail,” Alistair mused finally, addressing Paris directly now. “The dust, the dander, the sheer unreliability of organic matter. You are spending valuable time on what amounts to an exceptionally clean paw print.”

Paris didn't flinch under his scrutiny. She met his gaze without defensiveness. Her crimson glasses seemed to amplify the coolness of her focus. “Predictability is a construct of poor observation, Alistair,” she countered smoothly, letting the weight of his name fall like calculated mockery. “The genius of any system, whether electrical or social, lies not in its flawless architecture, but in how it manages the interference.”

She gestured with barely contained elegance toward the white cat, which seemed to be participating by slowly licking its immaculate paw, an act utterly devoid of self-consciousness.

Q caught the shift, sensing the professional tension building between Paris and Alistair. He quickly interjected, attempting to rein in the conversation before it spiraled into a philosophical debate on aesthetics versus actionable intel. “If we consider the cat's position—its continuous presence—it acts as a highly effective non-invasive signal dampener. A low-frequency, high-entropy background presence that may actually be crucial for keeping the primary receiver stable.” He sounded genuinely convinced of his own theory.

Paris ignored Q’s attempt at technical reclassification. Her attention remained anchored to the cat. It was not merely resting; it was *observing*. And its awareness—that cool, pale gaze fixed on her—was an interruption so perfect, so fundamentally random and unsophisticated in its target, that it tripped a switch somewhere deep within Paris's professional armor.

The realization settled over her with the quiet, undeniable weight of an inevitable conclusion: she was intellectually cornered by a single creature operating entirely outside the rules of physics or protocol. She should extract herself immediately; this level of uncontrolled, distracting whimsy jeopardized everything. But the residue on the dial, combined with the cat’s silent, steady presence, anchored her instead to the impossible question.

She had enough data now—the bio-polymers, the proximity anchor, the clear indicator that Laporte's system was sensitive to *existence*. And the cat merely sat there, a white punctuation mark of utter carelessness in the middle of their sophisticated machination.
