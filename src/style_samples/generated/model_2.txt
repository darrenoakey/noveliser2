Jeff’s breath hitched. The service door. Twelve feet of rusted steel and warped wood, hanging slightly ajar on corroded hinges. An exit. A coordinate. A way out of the bleed. His fingers locked around the clipboard. Laminate cold against his palm. Paper thick. A grid waiting to be filled. If he could just pin the coordinates of the threshold, he could map the escape. He could log the extraction. He could survive the variable. Safety lived in the margin. Safety lived in the ink. He moved. Boots scraping concrete. The hum spiked. Blue light slicing the dark. Rotors whining. Pitch climbing. Three drones. Descent from the rafters. Targeting reticles painting his chest. Red dots. Steady. Unblinking. He reached the door. Hand on the push bar. Rusted. Cold. He braced his shoulder. Pulled. Metal shrieked. Friction biting his skin.

The drones fired. Not bullets. Concussive pulses. Reality sheared. The wall behind him exploded inward. Drywall, insulation, framing—everything dissolved into a vortex of gray static and fragmented geometry. Shrapnel hissed past his ear. Heat blistered his neck. The air tasted of ozone and burnt wire. The static crawled down the ceiling. Peeling paint. Curling wallpaper. The house was peeling back. Exposing the bones beneath. Exposing him.

Jeff stumbled. Legs locked. The clipboard slipped. His thumb rubbed the binder’s edge. A grounding reflex. A habit carved into his bones. He dropped to a knee. Fumbled for his pocket. Pen. Where was the pen? The static brushed his boots. Numbing. Electric. Pulling the moisture from his skin. Pulling the rhythm from his heart. He needed the column. He needed the marginalia. He needed the proof that he had chosen the path. He looked up. The breach was tearing open. Concrete pillars groaned. Rebar snapped like dry twigs. The static roared. Not a sound. A physical weight. Multiversal chaos flooding the threshold. Fractured light. Shifting architecture. Screaming wind. Reality folding in on itself. Vanishing.

A shadow fell over him. Heavy. Imposing. Aris’s boot skidded into the concrete beside his boot. Mud-splattered. Unyielding. Her hand clamped over his wrist. Fingers like iron vices. Digging into the tendon. Poking bone. Jeff twisted. Torso spasming. Legs scrambling for purchase. The static lapped at his ankles. Cold. Icy. It didn’t burn. It connected. Grounding wires to flesh. Pulling him under. He yanked his arm. Muscles burning. The clipboard dangled from his fingers. The grid facing upward. Empty. Waiting. A void demanding ink. A void demanding proof.

“No,” he gasped. Throat tight. Words fracturing. “I need to log it. The coordinates are your nervous system, Jeff. The breach. I have to pin the—”

“Drop it.” Her voice cut through the hum. Dry. Metallic. Stripped of cadence. Uncompromising. She didn’t blink. Didn’t adjust her grip. Just leaned forward. Weight shifting. Shoulders locking. She drove her forearm into his bicep. Pressure mounting. Crushing the nerve. His fingers spasmed. The clipboard tumbled from his grip. Hit the concrete. Slid away into the pooling water. The grid disappeared into the dark. Into the noise. Into the unlogged.

The breach tore open. Full width. Full depth. The floor beneath them buckled. Water surged. Cold. Icy. Lapping at his shins. The static rushed in. Thick. Suffocating. It wasn’t just light anymore. It was sound. It was pressure. It was the screaming of a thousand collapsed timelines, all pressing against the membrane of his reality, all waiting to bleed through. The air grew heavy. Oppressive. It pressed against his lungs. Forced his ribs to compress. Made every breath a calculated risk. He reached back. Fingers stretching toward the fallen clipboard. Toward the pen. Toward the safety of the grid. The static brushed his fingertips. Numbing. Electric. Pulling him in. Pulling him apart.

Aris didn’t hesitate. Didn’t blink. Just hauled him up. One arm hooked under his shoulder. The other gripping the back of his collar. Fabric tearing. She dragged him backward. Away from the tear. Away from the noise. Away from the grid. Her boots hammered the concrete. Hard. Fast. Precise. Each step a dismissal. Each pull a demand. Jeff’s legs scrambled. Useless. Weightless. He thrashed. Reaching back. Fingers stretching toward the empty space where the clipboard had been. Toward the phantom weight of the pen. Toward the phantom safety of the columns. The static climbed higher. Up his thighs. His waist. His ribs. It wasn’t just cold anymore. It was hungry. It was consuming the coordinates. Consuming the baseline. Consuming him.

Aris kicked the push bar. The door groaned. Popped open. Cold air hit his face. Normal. Thin. Fragile. She shoved him through the gap. Shoulders first. Then her own boot. Clearing the threshold. The noise of the breach hit her back. A physical wave. Flattening the air. Folding the space behind them. Metal screamed. Drones dissolved into static. The hum flatlined into a deafening shriek. Reality folded in on itself. Vanished. The door slammed shut. Sealed by the pressure differential. The static pressed against the steel. Shuddering. Failing. Breaking through. But not here. Not now.

Aris released his collar. Stood tall. Breath steady. Eyes dark. Unblinking. She turned to him. Shoulders squared. Jaw set. The wind whipped her hair across her face. She didn’t look at the door. Looked at him. Jeff stood frozen. Chest heaving. Hands trembling. The clipboard was gone. The pen was gone. The grid was gone. The static still crawled up his legs. The noise still screamed in his ears. His mouth opened. Throat dry. Words failing. He reached into his pocket. Felt nothing. Just fabric. Smooth. Empty. The lie held his ribs together. The grid held his hands steady. Without it, he was raw. Exposed. A coordinate with no axis. A line with no anchor.

“You’re filing a breach,” Jeff said. Voice rough. Gravel in his throat. The words tumbled out. Fast. Desperate. A reflex. A plea. A prayer to a dead god. He stared at her hands. Empty. Unyielding. He stared at his own hands. Trembling. Unanchored. He felt the weight of the house behind him. The weight of the choice. The weight of the unlogged. It pressed down on his shoulders. Forced his spine to curve. Forced his knees to ache. He looked up. Met her eyes. Dark. Calculating. Not him. The room. The breach. The hum.

Aris stepped closer. Boots heavy on the asphalt. The noise of the breach muted behind them. Replaced by the quiet of the street. Distant cars. Birds. A normal Tuesday. The air tasted of exhaust and damp grass. Unfiltered. Unmapped. She looked at his empty hands. Looked at his face. Calm. Unyielding. Her voice dropped. Stripped of cadence. Stripped of mercy. 

“Reality doesn’t require a receipt.”

The words hit him like a physical blow. Dropped his shoulders. Dropped his breath. Dropped the last of his resistance. He stood there. Hollowed out. The static receded. Just an inch. Just enough to let the air back in. Just enough to let the world bleed back in. Normal. Fragile. Terrifying. He looked at the door. Looked at her. Looked at the street. The coordinates were gone. The grid was gone. The baseline was gone. He was just a man standing on asphalt. Waiting for the next variable. Waiting for the next choice. Waiting for the fall.

Aris turned. Nodded toward the street. “Move.”
