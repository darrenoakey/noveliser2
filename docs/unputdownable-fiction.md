## 1. Narrative Frameworks & Plot Typologies

A narrative must conform to structural models aligned with the cognitive rhythm
of beginning / middle / end. Booker's seven basic plots (Overcoming the Monster,
Rags to Riches, The Quest, Voyage and Return, Comedy, Tragedy, Rebirth) mirror
psychological maturity. Micro-narrative typologies shape subplots and
character-relationship beats:

- **Westergaard (leadership):** Trust, Teaching, Action, Values, Vision.
- **Masters (connection):** The Journey, The Challenge, The Discovery, The Vision, The Truth.
- **Sonata (pedagogy):** Problem-Action-Result, Cautionary Tale, Journey, Human Narrative, Vision.
- **Talkoclock:** Then & Now, I'm Not Perfect, It's Like…

Each micro-narrative serves a specific dramatic function (e.g. a Trust story of
vulnerability for the opening "save the cat" rapport; P-A-R for tight thriller
subplots; Cautionary Tale for antagonist origins / negative arcs).

## 2. The Character Engine — Wound → Lie → Want vs Need → Truth

- **Wound (Ghost):** a defining past pain (betrayal, abandonment, humiliation)
  convincing the character the world is dangerous.
- **Lie:** the survival belief built to shield the Wound — logical to the
  character, e.g. "I can only rely on myself; vulnerability leads to destruction."
  Distinguish Internal Lies (personal trauma) from External/Cultural Lies
  (society: "love is weakness").
- **Symptoms:** destructive behaviours caused by the Lie (isolation, control,
  guilt, shame) that block the external goal.
- **Want vs Need:** the external plot goal (Want) collides with the internal
  spiritual requirement (Need = the thematic Truth). The Lie first seems an asset,
  then becomes the primary obstacle.

**Arc types:** Positive Change (sheds the Lie, sacrifices Want for Need at the
climax), Flat (already holds the Truth; transforms a corrupt world), Disillusionment
(naive comfortable Lie → harsh Truth; wiser but broken), Corruption (holds the
Truth → seduced by a Lie → destroyed).

**Arc recipe:** pinpoint the Wound → formulate the Lie as one blunt sentence →
set Want vs Need → show Symptoms in Act I → crack the Lie in early Act II →
Moment of Truth at the Midpoint → in Act III force the choice to sacrifice Want
for Need.

## 3. Macro-Structure (Commercial Pacing Beats)

Allocate turning points by manuscript position:
- **Inciting Incident ~10%**
- **Plot Point One ~25%**
- **Midpoint ~50%** (Moment of Truth / mirror)
- **Plot Point Two ~75%**
- **Climax ~90%**

Alternative scaffolds: Save the Cat beats; 8-Stage Story Cycle (Stasis, Trigger,
Quest, Bolt, Shift, Defeat, Power, Resolution); 7-Point (Hook, Plot Turn 1,
Pinch 1, Midpoint, Pinch 2, Plot Turn 2, Climax). Logline: < 15 words, active
verbs, no character names — protagonist, conflict, stakes.

## 4. Micro-Structure — Scene & Sequel + Therefore/But

**Scene (proactive):** Goal → Conflict → Disaster (ends worse than it began).
**Sequel (reactive):** Reaction → Dilemma (all options constrained) → Decision
(sets the next Scene's Goal).

**Therefore/But causality:** connect beats with *therefore* (causation) or *but*
(conflict) — never "and then". Every action has a consequence; every success
meets an immediate complication.

## 5. The Prose Crucible — Microtension & Emotional Engineering

- **Microtension:** moment-by-moment suspense from *conflicting emotions within
  the scene*, not just plot threat.
- **Dialogue is compressed & subtextual:** characters rarely say what they mean;
  words hide a battle for control or safety. Not naturalistic.
- **Subjective description:** describe only what the POV character would notice
  under their *current emotional duress* (a frightened character notices the sharp
  angle of a desk, the cold of a key) — never neutral catalogue description.
- **Surprisingness:** genuine surprise spikes reader dopamine. Juxtapose
  contrasting emotions ("joyously sad" — laughter through tears) for composite,
  lingering feeling.
- **Show, don't name:** never write "she felt terrified". Reconstruct the concrete
  sensory inputs that trigger the emotion so the reader feels it.

**Physical pacing:**
- High-tension/action → short staccato sentences, fragments, stripped concrete vocabulary.
- Low-tension/reflective → long complex sentences, subordinate clauses, lyrical atmosphere.
- **Ticking clock:** a firm unavoidable deadline (literal or figurative) turns a
  goal into a desperate race.
- **Inverted triangle of constraints:** within each chapter the protagonist's
  options narrow from several to a single dangerous path; resolution opens a new
  triangle next chapter.

**Prose pitfalls to eliminate:**
- Passive voice & qualifiers (very, little, pretty, quite) → active verbs, delete qualifiers.
- Adverbial clutter (ran quickly) → one precise verb (sprinted).
- The "suddenly" crutch → write the abrupt action directly.
- Flabby flashbacks → weave brief vital memory into active sensory observation.
- Introspection overload → push the character into external action/dialogue.
- Weak dummy "there was…" → active concrete subject ("a cold wind swept the street").
- Choppy rhythmically-dead prose → vary sentence length, parallel construction.
- "-ing" participle overuse → sequential active verbs with a clear timeline.

## 6. Master Integration Workflow

1. **Concept:** one-sentence hook; Wound/Lie/Truth; macro beats at 10/25/50/75/90%.
2. **Schematic:** one-page character profiles (Want vs Need); scene list linked by
   Therefore/But; micro-narrative typologies on subplots.
3. **Draft:** Scene-Sequel per unit; subjective description; emotional surprisingness.
4. **Line edit:** vary sentence length to tension; ticking clock + constraint
   triangles; cleanse all prose pitfalls; parallel grammar for flow.
