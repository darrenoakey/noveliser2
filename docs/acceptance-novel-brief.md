# Acceptance novel brief: the impossible illusionist

## Immutable premise

A boy in a late-medieval/early-modern-like world is fascinated by a visiting travelling magician, runs away to join him, discovers every advertised feat is skilful ordinary illusion, and then discovers that *he alone* can do a real impossibility. Nobody accepts supernatural magic as a thing people can do. Trained stage illusionists are especially well placed to spot a violation of the trick's actual method. The boy must conceal his gift among them. This is a coming-of-age story across years, not a single-week adventure; his choices should grow in consequence until he achieves something genuinely great. The miracle is not public knowledge at the outset and should not turn the rest of the world into a conventional wizard society.

## Creative pressure, not boxes to tick

Keep the magician competent, specific and emotionally complicated, not a caricatured fraud. Make his stagecraft important to the boy's later survival and strategy. Give magic a concrete, fairly stable personal cost rather than a deus ex machina. The coming-of-age change must be enacted through decisions that cost something. A credible threat should emerge through the protagonist's earlier actions, not arrive from another genre. Relationship or romance belongs only if the other person has independent goals and can change the causal plot. The great act need not literally save the whole world, but must have earned public stakes and an irreversible private price. Show ordinary people with different reasons for doubting reported magic.

## Gates

1. Research and compare two independent plans/prose samples per shortlisted installed model with identical prompts; document side-by-side excerpts, failings and throughput.
2. Run the upgraded planning pipeline and inspect nonempty causal fields, actual links, setup/payoff and cast consistency; produce one or more short section pilots and reject hollow/contradictory scenes before continuing.
3. Only then produce the full EPUB, inspect structure and word count, read opening/midpoint/ending sections, verify premise/continuity/payoff and deliver files.
